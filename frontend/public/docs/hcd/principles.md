## What human-centered design means here

Human-centered design (HCD) is an approach to building products and services that starts with the people who will use them and keeps checking back with those people until the work is done. It is not a phase or a deliverable. It is a set of habits that make it more likely that what we ship is useful, usable and accessible to the public and to the staff who serve them.

The principles below follow ISO 9241-210, *Human-centred design for interactive systems*, adapted to how agency teams work. They apply to every product that uses this design system, whatever its size.

## The principles

### 1. Understand people, their tasks and their context

Design from an explicit understanding of who will use the product, what they are trying to get done and the conditions they work in: their devices, connectivity, time pressure, language, abilities and the rules that bind them.

- Describe users in terms of goals and circumstances, not demographics alone.
- Include the people who are hardest to reach: people with disabilities, people with limited English proficiency, people on old devices or slow connections, and people in rural areas.
- Remember staff users. Caseworkers, call-center agents and administrators use our products all day and their efficiency shapes the public's experience.

### 2. Involve users throughout design and development

Involve real users from the first idea to after launch, not just at a usability test before release. Short, frequent sessions beat one large study.

- Talk to users before deciding what to build.
- Test early concepts, even paper sketches.
- Keep a channel open after launch: feedback forms, support tickets and analytics are research data.

### 3. Let evaluation with users drive and refine the design

Decisions are tested with users and refined based on what is learned, not settled by opinion or seniority. When the team disagrees, run a study.

- Agree in advance what success looks like (task completion, time on task, errors, satisfaction).
- Evaluate against those measures and change the design when it falls short.
- Record what was tested and what changed, so later teams don't repeat the work.

### 4. Iterate

Expect the first design to be wrong in places. Plan for several rounds of design, evaluation and change, and size releases so that the team can learn from each one.

### 5. Design the whole experience

A person's experience includes everything around the screen: how they heard about the service, the letter they received, the call they made, the documents they had to find and what happens after they press Submit. Design for the whole journey, across channels and across the offices that own each part.

### 6. Bring different skills and perspectives to the team

HCD needs researchers, designers, content designers, engineers, accessibility specialists, policy and program staff, and people with lived experience of the service. Each sees problems the others miss.

## Principles in practice

| Instead of | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Building to the requirements document | Validate the requirements with users first; they describe a solution, not a need |
| Testing once before launch | Test small and often from the first sketch |
| Designing for the "average user" | Design for the edges; it works better for everyone |
| Measuring outputs (features shipped) | Measure outcomes (tasks completed, time saved, errors avoided) |
| Treating accessibility as a final check | Build it into research, design and code from the start |
| Writing for the agency | Write in plain language for the reader |

## How the principles connect to policy

Federal requirements point in the same direction:

- The **21st Century Integrated Digital Experience Act** (2018) requires public-facing websites and digital services to be accessible, consistent, user-centered and mobile-friendly, among other things.
- **Executive Order 14058** (2021) directs agencies to design and deliver services with a focus on the experience of the people they serve.
- **OMB Circular A-11, Section 280** sets out how agencies manage customer experience and measure it.
- **Section 508** of the Rehabilitation Act requires information and communication technology to be accessible to people with disabilities. See [Accessibility](/hcd/accessibility).
