## What the council does

The HCD council is a cross-agency group of practitioners who support teams in applying human-centered design and keep research and design practice consistent across programs. It exists to help teams, not to add a gate: most of its work is advice, review and sharing what other teams have learned.

The council:

- **Reviews research plans** for larger studies, especially those that involve the public, sensitive topics or Paperwork Reduction Act clearance, and helps teams get them right before recruiting.
- **Reviews findings and design direction** at key points in larger efforts, so decisions rest on evidence.
- **Sets shared practice:** research ethics and consent, data handling, accessibility in research, and templates such as research plans, discussion guides and reports.
- **Shares knowledge** across programs, so teams can build on research others have already done instead of repeating it.
- **Advises on the design system** where research shows a component or pattern isn't working for people, through the design system's [review process](/review).

## Who is on it

Membership should cover the skills that HCD needs. A typical council includes:

| Role | Brings |
| --- | --- |
| User researchers | Research methods, ethics, synthesis |
| Designers (interaction, service, content) | Design practice and the design system |
| Accessibility specialists | Section 508 and WCAG expertise; research with people with disabilities |
| Engineers | Feasibility and how designs are built |
| Product and program staff | Program goals, policy constraints and delivery |
| Privacy and information collection advisors (as needed) | Privacy Act and Paperwork Reduction Act guidance |

Members rotate so that different programs are represented over time. Anyone practicing HCD at the agency can attend open sessions.

## When to bring work to the council

Bring work early, when advice can still change it. Good moments:

- **Before a study** that involves members of the public, a sensitive topic, a new recruiting channel or PRA clearance.
- **After discovery**, to test the problem statement and research findings before design begins.
- **Before a major release**, to review usability and accessibility evidence.
- **When research shows a problem with a shared component or pattern**, so that the fix reaches every product that uses it.

Small studies and routine usability testing don't need council review. Use the guidance on this site and ask a council member if you are unsure.

## How to bring work

1. **Prepare a short summary:** the decision you are facing, what you have learned so far, and what you want from the council (advice, a review, a decision).
2. **Attach your materials:** a research plan (see [User research](/hcd/research)), findings or a report (see [Research reports](/hcd/research-reports)), or prototypes.
3. **Submit a request** through the [review process](/review) or [get in touch](/getting-started/get-in-touch) with the design system team, who will route it to the council.
4. **Present for 15 to 20 minutes** and leave time for discussion.

## What you get back

Each review ends with a written summary in the request, so the outcome is on record:

- **Go ahead:** the work is sound; any suggestions are optional.
- **Go ahead with changes:** specific changes the council recommends, with the reason for each.
- **Come back:** the council needs more information or evidence before it can advise.

Advice from the council is grounded in the evidence presented. Where members disagree, the summary says so.

## Principles the council works by

- **Evidence over opinion.** Recommendations cite research, standards or prior findings.
- **People first.** The experience of the public and of front-line staff comes before internal convenience.
- **Accessible by default.** Accessibility is part of every review, not a separate one.
- **Proportionate.** Light-touch for small work, more thorough for high-risk or high-reach work.
- **Open.** Summaries and shared templates are available to every team.
