## The purpose of a report

A research report turns sessions and notes into findings that a team can act on, and keeps a record that outlives the project. Write it for the people who will make decisions, not for other researchers: lead with what was learned and what to do about it.

Not every study needs a long document. Match the format to the audience:

| Format | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| Top-line summary (one page) | Within a day or two of the last session, to keep the team moving |
| Full report | Larger studies, findings that inform policy or funding, or work other teams will build on |
| Highlight reel | Short video clips of participants that make a problem real for stakeholders (only with consent for that use) |
| Workshop | When the team needs to decide together what to do next |

## Structure of a full report

1. **Summary.** The three to five most important findings and recommendations, in a few paragraphs. Many readers will stop here.
2. **Background and goals.** The decision the research was informing and the research questions.
3. **Method.** What you did, when, and with which tools or prototypes.
4. **Participants.** Who took part and how they were recruited, described without identifying anyone. Note who was missing.
5. **Findings.** Each finding with its evidence and severity (see below).
6. **Recommendations.** What to do about each finding, with an owner where possible.
7. **Limitations.** What the study can't tell you: sample size, missing groups, prototype fidelity.
8. **Appendix.** Discussion guide, tasks, screener and raw measures.

## Writing findings

A good finding describes what happened, how often, why it matters and the evidence for it.

> **People could not find where to update their mailing address (high severity).**
> 6 of 8 participants looked under "Contact us" before "My profile." Four gave up and said they would call. *"I'd expect it to be with the phone number, not with my password."* (P3)

- **Be specific.** "The address field caused errors" is less useful than "people entered their apartment number in the street line and the form rejected it."
- **Show the evidence.** Counts from qualitative studies ("6 of 8") and short, de-identified quotes. Don't turn small samples into percentages.
- **Separate observation from interpretation.** Say what you saw, then what you think it means.
- **Include what worked.** Teams need to know what to keep.

### Severity

Rate usability problems so that teams fix the most important first. A simple scale:

| Severity | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Prevents people from completing the task, or causes serious harm (wrong payment, lost data) |
| High | Causes significant delay, frustration or errors; many people affected |
| Medium | Causes some difficulty; people recover |
| Low | Minor irritation or cosmetic |

Accessibility barriers that stop someone using a service are always critical, however few participants hit them.

## Recommendations

- Tie each recommendation to a finding.
- Say what to change, not just what is wrong, but leave room for the team to design the solution.
- Prioritize, and note effort where you can.
- Distinguish quick fixes from questions that need more research.

## Privacy and accessibility

- Use participant numbers, never names. Remove details that could identify someone indirectly.
- Get consent before sharing video or audio clips, and only for the uses participants agreed to.
- Publish reports in accessible formats. An accessible web page or document meets Section 508 more reliably than a slide deck exported to PDF. Give charts text alternatives and data tables.

## Sharing and storing reports

- Present findings to the team and stakeholders; reports read alone are often skimmed.
- Add the report to the [HCD reports library](/research/hcd-reports) and record the study in [User studies](/research/user-studies) so other teams can find it.
- Add the key findings to [Research insights](/research/insights) with a category and priority, so they can be tracked across studies.
- Come back to the report after the next release: which recommendations were made, and did they work?
