## Introducing something new

People come to the product to get work done, not to learn about features. Introduce new things in the moment they become useful.

- Prefer a short, dismissible pointer next to the new control over a tour that blocks the page.
- Show an introduction once. Remember that it was dismissed, across devices if you can.
- Explain the benefit ("Filter by owner to see only your work"), not the feature name.
- Link to the full documentation or changelog for people who want more.

## Patterns to choose from

- **Badge**: a small "New" label on a menu item or tab. Remove it after the person has visited the feature, or after a few weeks.
- **Inline callout**: a note in the page flow that explains a change to a familiar screen.
- **Pointer**: a popover anchored to the new control, for one key feature at a time.
- **What's new page**: a dated list of changes, always available, for everything else.

## What to avoid

- Several pointers at once, or a tour on someone's first visit before they have seen the product.
- Modals that announce features; they interrupt and are dismissed unread.
- Moving familiar controls without saying where they went.

## Accessibility

- Make every pointer and callout reachable by keyboard and closable with Escape.
- Don't move focus to an announcement unless the person asked for it.
- Give "New" badges text that screen readers read in context ("Reports, new").
